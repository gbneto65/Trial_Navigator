Help & User Guide

Trial Navigator helps technical teams plan and size trials that prove product performance in real farms and research centers—so adoption and sales follow.

Important note

Pop. Average is shown for context; results are based on absolute differences (Expected Δ and Min Δ) and their variability. Changing Pop. Average does not affect the Commercial readout or Power.

Business & Sales use

The Commercial readout estimates the chance that your observed difference will meet the business success threshold (Min Δ) in customer experiences. These are practical trials where farmers test a product in their own operations before deciding to buy. It’s common to set Min Δ as the minimum performance improvement needed to offset the product investment (product cost, application, logistics) and meet your ROI expectations.

  • If Min Δ ≥ 0 (desired increase): success means (Treatment − Control) ≥ Min Δ.
  • If Min Δ < 0 (desired decrease): success means (Treatment − Control) ≤ Min Δ.

Use the suggested sample sizes (70/80/90%) to build trials - the standard power is 80%.

Inputs

Pop. Average: Baseline mean before any treatment. (Used for context only in the current model.)

Std. Dev. (σ): Shared standard deviation assumed equal across groups.

Expected Δ: Mean difference you expect (Treatment − Control). Positive = increase; negative = decrease.

Min Δ (success threshold): The smallest improvement that counts as success in business terms (zero and negative values allowed).

Sample Size (n): Per group (control and treatment).

Tail: One‑tailed if change is only meaningful in one direction; Two‑tailed if either direction matters.

α (p‑value threshold): Evidence threshold (0.10 lenient, 0.05 typical, 0.01 strict).

Bonferroni (α/m): When there are m primary endpoints, divide α by m to control overall false positives. Recommended: apply Bonferroni primarily in research trials and formal experiments with multiple pre‑specified primary endpoints where strong family‑wise error control is required.

Assumptions

  • Continuous outcome reasonably normal distribution.
  • Similar SD across groups (pooled equal‑variance t‑test).
  • Independence of observations; equal per‑group n.
